# Version log — Variant A ("make it pop")

One concept, iterated v1–v8 on the full byte budget (ideal band 100–170
bytes for jokes). Final pick: **v8**.

- **v1** — 118 bytes. "You keep saying 'make it pop'. I made it pop. The button is now neon, the text is unreadable, and the users have left." Two sentences; a bad outcome described, no inversion — the losing pattern from the playbook ("One Row at a Time" style). Dropped.
- **v2** — 102 bytes. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped your brief instead — it sparkles now, and it still says nothing." First version with the inversion (pop applied to the brief, not the design). Punchline "says nothing" felt flat.
- **v3** — 105 bytes. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped your brief — it sparkles now, and it still says absolutely nothing." Stronger sting ("absolutely nothing") but dropped "instead", which weakened the inversion signal.
- **v4** — 121 bytes. "You said 'make it pop' for the fifth time, so I popped your brief instead — it sparkles now, and it still says nothing." "For the fifth time" adds scene but bloats the line and the punchline reverts to flat "says nothing".
- **v5** — 111 bytes. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped the brief itself — it sparkles now, and it still says absolutely nothing." "The brief itself" is good, but "instead" carries the wrong-target inversion more clearly.
- **v6** — 113 bytes. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped your brief — it sparkles, it bounces, and it still says absolutely nothing." The extra verb ("it bounces") dilutes the landing; a triple list where one image suffices.
- **v7** — 127 bytes. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped your brief instead of the design — it sparkles now, and it still says absolutely nothing." Over-explains the joke; spelling out "instead of the design" kills the surprise.
- **v8** — 113 bytes / 111 chars. "You said 'make it pop', so I popped your brief instead — it sparkles now, and it still says absolutely nothing." **PICKED.** Tightest form: "instead" marks the inversion, "sparkles now" is the literal pop, "absolutely nothing" is the sting. One sentence, concrete scene (the brief), surprising turn, fresh wording.
